    How the comparisons differ

    • Presidential vs midterm: 2024 was a presidential election, so nearly every state trails it. 2022 and 2018 (midterms) are the fairer comparisons.
    • Different measures: Florida, Texas (so far) and Michigan are mostly mail ballots because early in-person voting hasn't opened. North Carolina, Kansas and Ohio include early in-person voting, which started earlier in some states this year than in past years.
    • Different calendars: early voting start dates moved between cycles (NC started Oct 15 this year vs Oct 20 in 2022; Iowa cut early voting from 29 to 20 days in 2021). A state can look far ahead or behind just because its window opened on a different day.
    • Reporting lags: Maine's latest file is from Oct 13, Ohio's from Oct 13; most others are from Oct 15–16. Each state is compared at its own latest day. Texas's 2026 statewide mail count has barely moved in recent days (38,050 → 38,054), which may be a reporting lag rather than a real drop.
    • Law changes: Ohio (SB 293) and Kansas (SB 4) now require mail ballots to arrive by Election Day; Maine opened its ongoing absentee list to everyone; Michigan created a permanent absentee list in 2022; Florida's SB 90 (2021) made voters re-request mail ballots more often. Details are on each state page.
    • Combined total: only states with an exact same-day statewide match are added up, and big states (Florida, Michigan) dominate the sum.
