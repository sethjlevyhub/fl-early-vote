Comparisons line up 2026 with past elections at the same number of days before Election Day. Each state counts something slightly different, so check the state rows before comparing totals. Methods & sources
Not a national sample. This page combines only the 16 states this dashboard tracks that have 2026 data (AZ, CO, FL, GA, IA, KS, ME, MI, MN, NC, NV, OH, PA, TX, VA, WI). They were picked for competitive races and good data, not to represent the country. Each state counts different things (mail only, mail plus in person), reports on different schedules, and changed its laws between cycles. Read the state-by-state bars first; the combined total is a rough summary.

    How the comparisons differ

    • Presidential vs midterm: 2024 was a presidential election, so nearly every state trails it. 2022 and 2018 (midterms) are the fairer comparisons.
    • Different measures: Florida, Texas and Michigan counts are mostly mail ballots until in-person early voting opens statewide (Texas Oct 19; Florida and Michigan Oct 24). North Carolina, Kansas and Ohio include early in-person voting, which started earlier in some states this year than in past years.
    • Different calendars: early voting start dates moved between cycles (NC started Oct 15 this year vs Oct 20 in 2022; Iowa cut early voting from 29 to 20 days in 2021). A state can look far ahead or behind just because its window opened on a different day.
    • Reporting lags: each state posts on its own schedule (dates in the bar at the top), and each state is compared at its own latest day.
    • Law changes: Ohio (SB 293) and Kansas (SB 4) now require mail ballots to arrive by Election Day; Maine opened its ongoing absentee list to everyone; Michigan created a permanent absentee list in 2022; Florida's SB 90 (2021) made voters re-request mail ballots more often. Details are on each state page.
    • Virginia uses secondary sources (marked †): the official ELECT Daily Absentee List is sold, not posted, so 2022 comes from VPAP and 2024/2026 from EPEC/Digital Poll Watchers, both built from that file. Pennsylvania is mail only (no in-person early voting) and has no 2018 comparison because no-excuse mail voting began in 2020.
    • Colorado (†): 2026 from civicAPI until the state posts its daily workbooks; past years are official but start 14–15 days out, and 2026 ballots went out a week earlier (HB26-1113). Oregon has a page but isn't counted here yet: its 2026 report hasn't been posted.
    • Nevada uses secondary sources (†): 2026 from civicAPI; past totals are figures Jon Ralston stated, which begin 15 days out (2024) and 10 days out (2022), so Nevada has no same-day comparison yet.
    • Minnesota and Wisconsin use secondary sources (†) for 2026 (civicAPI, from the state files; the official sites block automated downloads). Minnesota past points are weekly, so it is compared with the nearest past day. Wisconsin has no 2018 comparison.
    • Arizona uses secondary sources: there is no official statewide daily report in 2026. 2018 comes from the Secretary of State's daily update as posted by Garrett Archer, 2022/2024 from Sam Almy's tracker built from county return files (rounded figures), and 2026 from Stealth Analytics' totals of county files (a few county files are 1–8 days old). All four count ballots received by counties, so Arizona is included in the combined totals, marked †.
    • Combined total: only states with an exact same-day statewide match are added up, and big states (Florida, Michigan) dominate the sum.
