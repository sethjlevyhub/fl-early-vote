    Sources & caveats

      • Manual updates. The SOS file sits behind a bot check, so the box can't download it. The page shows the date of the last file downloaded by hand; it won't refresh until a new file is loaded.
      • No race or age in the official file. The SOS absentee file has no race, sex or birth year. The race and age charts use georgiavotes.com, an unofficial site that joins the SOS file to the purchased voter list (not affiliated with the SOS). Its totals match the official file within a few ballots (552,066 vs 552,062). The box saves its summary daily, so daily race shares will build from Oct 16 on.
      • No party. Georgia doesn't register voters by party.
      • Past cycles are thin until the official past files are loaded. They're SOS figures quoted in news coverage for the first two days of early voting. Early voting started on a different date in each cycle, so they're compared by early-voting day, not days out.
      • Counts are accepted ballots (status A) with a return date, from STATEWIDE.csv only; the zip also has per-county copies, which would double-count. "Mail" includes 2,163 electronic (UOCAVA) ballots. 2024 was a presidential year.

      Law changes that affect comparisons

      Rule changes between cycles can shift how (not just how many) people vote early. Keep these in mind when comparing to past years.

      • 2021 · SB 202: mail ballots can be requested no earlier than 11 weeks out (was 180 days) and no later than 11 days before the election; mail requests and ballots require ID (driver's license number or other ID); drop boxes limited to early-voting sites during voting hours, at most one per 100,000 active voters; a second Saturday of early voting required; runoffs cut from 9 to 4 weeks; no unsolicited mail-ballot applications from governments. In effect for 2022 and 2024; 2018 was under the old rules. Source · Summary
      • 2024 · SB 189: eased voter challenges, set chain-of-custody rules and faster reporting deadlines for absentee and early ballots, and required tabulating from ballot text instead of QR codes. A June 2026 special-session law (SB 3EX) pushed the QR-code deadline to Jan 1, 2028 and left absentee and early-voting deadlines unchanged for 2026. SB 189 text · SB 3EX explainer
